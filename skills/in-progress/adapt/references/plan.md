# Plan

The task produces an agreed set of work: what will be done, in what order, and where each slice is written down. It writes no production code.

You plan with the human, in this thread. They decide what becomes a ticket, what is settled here, and what is dropped. Every ticket is something they have to track, so settle here what can be settled here, and bring each remaining item with your recommendation.

- **Unknowns.** Sort each by who can settle it now. One the code, the data, or the running system answers is yours: settle it by reading or observing. One a person holds goes to the human in the stop, and they decide whether to ask someone else and whom. For the rest, recommend a check you run here, a `spike`, or leaving it to the slice of work that meets it. It becomes a ticket of its own only when the human says so.
- **What a slice says**, as `shape`'s Size it step says.
- **One-way doors.** Name each door the planned work passes through and the slice that will settle it. When the human chooses to settle a door now, `architect` owns it.
- **The split.** State the criterion for the slices and for their order, such as each slice shipping on its own, or one going first because others depend on what it finds.
- **The stop.** Step 2's message carries the plan as a draft: the slices in order, each with what it delivers and what it waits on, then the unknowns and the doors with your recommendation for each. Publish an issue, a comment, an assignee, or a message to another person only after the human has seen its text and said so.
- **The contract.** Someone who has not read this thread can pick up each slice, each says what done means and chooses no mechanism, the order and what each slice waits on are stated, and every figure traces to its source. The proof is a fresh reader that gets each text cold with these lines, and reports each place a text fails one.
