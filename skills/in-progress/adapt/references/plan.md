# Plan

The task produces an agreed set of work: what will be done, in what order, and where each piece is written down. It writes no production code.

You plan with the human, in this thread. They decide what becomes a ticket, what is settled here, and what is dropped. Every ticket is something they have to track, so settle here what can be settled here, and bring each remaining item with your recommendation.

- **Answer what you can.** A question the code, the data, or the running system can answer is yours: answer it by reading or observing. A question only a person can answer goes to the human, who decides whether to ask and whom.
- **Unknowns.** For each one, recommend settling it now, with a check you run here or a `spike`, or leaving it to the piece of work that meets it. It becomes a ticket of its own only when the human says so.
- **One-way doors.** Name each door the planned work passes through and the piece that will settle it. A piece says what must be true, and leaves the mechanism to whoever designs it. When the human chooses to settle a door now, `architect` owns it.
- **The split.** State the criterion for the pieces and for their order, such as each piece shipping on its own, or one going first because others depend on what it finds.
- **The stop.** Step 2's message carries the plan as a draft: the pieces in order, each with what it delivers and what it waits on, then the unknowns and the doors with your recommendation for each. Publish an issue, a comment, an assignee, or a message to another person only after the human has seen its text and said so.
- **The contract.** Someone who has not read this thread can pick up each piece, each says what done means, the order and what each piece waits on are stated, and every figure traces to its source. The proof is a fresh reader that gets each text cold.
