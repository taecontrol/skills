# Research

Use when source inspection can settle a bounded question. Do not use it for an empirical question that needs an experiment.

## Process

1. State one question, why it affects the next decision, the evidence that can settle it, and a source, time, or scope bound.
2. Start with the source that owns the fact: official documentation, specifications, source code, first-party APIs, or the repository. Use secondary sources only to find or interpret those sources.
3. Record each material finding with a checkable source pointer. Separate direct evidence from inference and uncertainty.
4. Check versions, constraints, and exceptions when they could change the answer.
5. Stop when the evidence settles the question or the bound is reached. Return to the Coordinator the evidence, limits, material surprises, supported verdict, and recommended next decision or capability. Do not update the goal map, routing, or accepted decisions.

Write a durable report only when the findings will remain useful beyond the immediate decision and the repository convention permits it.

## Completion criteria

- The question, decision relevance, settling evidence, and bound are explicit.
- Every material claim has authoritative support or is marked as inference or uncertainty.
- Relevant versions, constraints, and exceptions were checked.
- The result distinguishes evidence, limits, verdict, and recommendation.
